# Playlist Scoring

> A playlist is not a collection. It is an edit.

Phase 3A scores one candidate at a time. It deliberately does not construct a
playlist. The score is evidence that a later sequencer can use while enforcing
duration, repetition, role, and narrative constraints.

## Song quality and experience quality

A listener may love a song that is wrong for a particular moment. Preference
captures individual song affinity; context and phase scores describe whether
that song supports the requested listening experience. Keeping those components
separate prevents general taste from silently overriding the journey's purpose.

For Active Focus, the context component rewards declared context fit,
instrumentalness, and groove while reducing credit for lyrical distraction. The
phase component compares track energy with the phase's intended energy arc.

## Local transitions and global narrative

Transition continuity is local: it asks whether energy, groove, and emotion make
sense immediately after the previous track. Intentional contrast can make a
sharp local change coherent, so contrast reduces the abrupt-transition penalty
rather than automatically making every discontinuity good.

Narrative continuity is global: it asks whether the candidate still serves the
playlist's larger direction. Phase 3A accepts an explicit narrative-drift
penalty so that future planning code can express this distinction without
hiding it inside transition quality.

## Genre is not a required dimension

Genre labels are broad catalog groupings, not reliable transition rules. Tracks
from different genres can share energy, groove, emotional direction, and
narrative purpose; tracks from the same genre can clash on all four. The scorer
therefore evaluates continuity directly and never requires genre similarity.

## Explainable components

The positive score is a deterministic 0–100 weighted sum:

| Component | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Listener preference | 25% |
| Context fit | 25% |
| Phase and energy fit | 20% |
| Transition quality | 20% |
| Gated discovery value | 10% |

Constraint and narrative-drift penalties are then subtracted and remain visible
in the result. Discovery credit is gated by context, phase, and transition fit,
so novelty cannot rescue a disruptive candidate.

Role changes the scoring contract:

- An opening anchor treats familiarity as valuable and visibly flags high
  novelty or distracting lyrics. It receives full transition credit when there
  is no predecessor because no incoming transition is required.
- A journey track uses the default weights above.
- A discovery spotlight shifts 10 percentage points from preference to
  discovery value. Novelty receives that credit only after minimum context,
  phase, and transition thresholds pass.

A candidate without a predecessor receives neutral transition credit unless it
is an opening anchor.

## Penalty boundaries

A weak transition loses positive transition credit. Only a severe energy and
groove discontinuity below the centralized abruptness threshold receives an
additional constraint penalty; this represents the distinct failure of crossing
a hard disruption boundary, not merely being less smooth. Intentional contrast
reduces that surcharge.

Narrative drift is never inferred from the same local transition profile. It is
an explicit caller-supplied penalty reserved for evidence that the candidate
departs from the journey's global direction. With no such evidence it remains
zero, including for an abrupt local transition. This prevents one discontinuity
from being charged under transition, constraint, and narrative labels.

Every breakdown also includes concise rule-generated reasons. These reasons are
fixed consequences of scoring inputs, not text generated by an AI provider.
Component scores and explanations make tuning auditable and allow the eventual
sequencer to explain why one candidate beat another.
